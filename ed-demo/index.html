The Shift · From Library Floor to AI

Who is doing the thinking?

Finding information kept getting faster. Weighing conflicting evidence still belonged to the student, until the finished paper started arriving before the thinking.

1. Before the internet

Research at the library, books in the card catalog, handwritten notes spread out on the floor. Constructing an argument took real critical thinking at every level.

"Where does this card go?"
2. Research moves online

Sources available in seconds, citing moves from library books to websites, and notes are haphazard. Critical thinking remains an important part of the process, but it's easier to grab ideas and put them together than it used to be.

"Wait. That is one source."
3. Early shortcuts

A lifted paragraph, a downloaded essay, and an honest paper land in the same tray. The trust gap starts before chatbots.

"Which student weighed the evidence?"
4. Back to blue books

With AI writing full essays, schools close laptops and return to paper exams. Tools from 2026, assignments from 1997.

The ExComm Decision Memo.

Ms. Alvarez assigns a classic historical dilemma designed to force genuine judgment before students know how the crisis ended.

MS. ALVAREZ · PERIOD 3 PROMPT 500 WORDS · DUE THURSDAY
Your role: You are an advisor to President Kennedy inside the Executive Committee on October 20, 1962. U-2 photos confirm Soviet nuclear missiles in Cuba. Recommend either an immediate airstrike or a naval quarantine using the two primary memos. No hindsight allowed.
1State what each side argues in Documents A and B
2Weigh the competing risks and evidence
3Commit to one clear recommendation
4Answer the strongest objection to your call
U-2 RECONNAISSANCE OVERLAY · CUBA READINESS: DAYS
Document A · Strike Case

Joint Chiefs & Dean Acheson

A blockade gives Soviet crews time to arm missiles already on the island. Yet the Air Force warns strikes cannot guarantee 100% destruction, and Moscow could retaliate against West Berlin.

Document B · Quarantine Case

McNamara & Robert Kennedy

A surprise attack on a small neighbor looks like "Pearl Harbor in reverse" and corners Khrushchev into nuclear escalation. A naval quarantine buys time while keeping military force available.

The Wisest Option

In October 1962, the world stood closer to nuclear war than at any moment in history. Faced with Soviet missiles in Cuba, President Kennedy had to weigh decisive military action against measured restraint. This memo argues that a naval blockade was the wisest option because a blockade was firm, flexible, and morally defensible.

First, the blockade demonstrated American resolve without closing the door to diplomacy. The policy sent a clear message to Moscow while giving both superpowers room to step back from the brink. Furthermore, a quarantine avoided the catastrophic risks of a surprise attack.

  • Send Ms. Alvarez a compact trail matching her 4 steps, blocked shortcut attempts, and 1 oral follow-up question. Keep the full chat private.
The New Week · How Students Start

Students tap their own AI into the shared server.

When students sit down to do the assignment, they instruct their AI to tap into Ms. Alvarez's server. That feeds her instructions to their AI and lets the AI report back how they worked. They can use whichever AI platform they prefer, and if they skip connecting, Ms. Alvarez sees no work notes.
